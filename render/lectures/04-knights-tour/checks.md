# Durlabh #04 · A knight's tour in a Sanskrit verse: three checks (24 Nov 2026)

1. Own check: Murthy, Resonance 25(8) 2020, 1095–1116 (IAS full text and the vishvasa.github.io copy): verse 5.15 of the Kāvyālaṅkāra in Devanagari and transliteration; Rudraṭa "around the ninth century"; translation "I, a truthful well-read man, a leader of a group, helpful to servants, praise the army which has as its leader a man who praises playful persons"; Namisādhu's key verse and the थ/ध error; path in Fig. 12; Haravijaya "the earliest of the poetic works"; Deśika's pair 30-19/30-20. Knuth, TAOCP Vol. 4 Pre-Fascicle 8A (draft 4 Dec 2025), pp. 3–5, 9: Rudraṭa c. 815, sloka 15 in chapter 5, sloka 14 a rook's tour; "Two copies of Rudraṭa's half-tour will make an open tour on the full board"; "two knight's tours consistent with his sloka"; Ratnākara c. 830, "a better idea a few years later", two copies rotated make a closed tour; al-ʿAdlī 842 (Murray 1913, 175–176, 336); "half of a chessboard, 4 × 8, had been published even earlier, by Kashmiri poets"; Bhoja c. 1050; Pādukāsahasra 1313; Euler, Mémoires Berlin 15 (1759), 310–337. IIIT Bangalore Pādukā page: Deśika's first śloka written sequentially, the second read off along the tour. Python: 32 syllables, 4 distinct; the path is 32 knight moves visiting each square once, reproduces the verse, open.
2. Subagent A (with sources): confirmed the verse, counts, translation word for word, 5.15 and 5.14, dates, Knuth quotes, key and थ/ध error, path (rebuilt from the corrected key: matches), Bhoja, Deśika pair, Euler citation, Resonance details. Not confirmed: glosses "the ornaments of poetry" and "the horse's step"; "chapter 5 deals with citrakāvya"; rook "far easier"; "Rudraṭa does not draw the path"; "in a manuscript" (Murthy: printing or copying); "al-ʿAdlī's tours" plural (Knuth: closed tour by al-ʿAdlī, open by Ibn Manīʿ); Ratnākara "cleverer" reason conflated (Knuth's better idea is the two-verse scheme).
3. Subagent B (no sources): nothing proven false; computed two consistent paths, both open. Doubtful: rook comparison; Ratnākara reason; "Rāma's sandals" (the hymn praises Raṅganātha's pādukās; Murthy says Śrīrāma's: sources disagree); "Euler made it a mathematical problem in Europe" (de Moivre 1722 and earlier European tours); "paper of 1759" (volume dated 1759, printed 1766); "nine centuries" read as Knuth's; Ratnākara wrote a pair of verses; who came first (Knuth: Rudraṭa; Murthy: Ratnākara); Deśika "around 1300" vs Knuth 1313.

Changes made (rule: keep only what both confirm):
- Glosses of Kāvyālaṅkāra and turaga-pada removed ("turaga means a horse" kept, per Murthy). "Picture poetry" → "figure poetry" (Murthy). Chapter line → "Rudraṭa discusses citrakāvya in detail".
- Rook line reduced to Knuth's statement. "Rudraṭa does not draw the path" → "The path is supplied by ... Namisādhu". "In a manuscript" → "in copying or printing".
- Earliest tours: al-ʿAdlī's closed tour (book dated 842) and Ibn Manīʿ's open tour, as Knuth puts it.
- Ratnākara: a pair of verses; Knuth's "better idea" = two verses; closed tour from two rotated copies kept as a separate note. New paragraph: who came first is not agreed (Knuth vs Murthy).
- Pādukāsahasra: "a devotional hymn on the Lord's sandals" (whose sandals is disputed between sources); date given as Knuth's 1313.
- Euler: "wrote the classic mathematical paper ... in volume 15 of the Mémoires ..., dated 1759"; no claim that the problem began with him. "Nine centuries" made our own statement in the summary.
- Haravijaya canto left out (Knuth 43.145–146, Murthy canto 48).
