# Durlabh #08 · The Linguistic Survey's gramophone records: India's voices, 1913–1929 (Sun 10 Jan 2027) · Linguistics

Angle: between 1913 and 1929, as a supplement to George Grierson's Linguistic Survey of India, provincial governments had speakers of India's languages and dialects recorded on gramophone discs by representatives of the Gramophone Company, Calcutta. One of the standard specimens was a translation of the Parable of the Prodigal Son, the same passage printed for every dialect in the Survey's volumes, so the discs let you hear one story told across dozens of tongues. 242 discs are in the British Library (India Office Records IOR/S/2); the Digital South Asia Library (University of Chicago) has put the audio and transcripts online. Open on one voice (a named Delhi speaker from 1920, if confirmed in the catalogue or DSAL itself), then: how the recordings were made, which provinces took part and which did not, what a hundred-year-old recording can and cannot tell a linguist, and how to listen yourself.

Leads found 24 Nov 2026 (confirm everything in the three checks):
- British Library catalogue, IOR/S/2 "Linguistic Survey of India Gramophone Records", 242 records, 1913–1929 (first late 1913 or early 1914; last late 1928 or early 1929, Assam); made by representatives of the Gramophone Company, Calcutta; Kashmir, the Punjab and the North West Frontier did not take part; three specimens per language, one the Prodigal Son (citing LSI vol. 1, p. 18). https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/036-000091073
- Digital South Asia Library, About the Linguistic Survey of India: printed 1903–1928; 179 languages and 544 dialects; recordings 1913–1929 as a supplement. https://dsal.uchicago.edu/books/lsi/about-lsi.html
- Grierson, Linguistic Survey of India, vol. I, part I, Introductory (Calcutta, 1927): the primary account of the recordings (find the page in the DSAL scan).
- Gramophone Records of Languages and Dialects spoken in the Central Provinces and Berar (Madras, 1920), p. vii (cited by the BL).
- Center for Research Libraries note on the DSAL digitisation (originals at the British Library Sound Archive; "nearly 100" languages and dialects). https://www.crl.edu/node/6790
