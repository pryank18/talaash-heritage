# Durlabh #06 · The Delhi Urdu Akhbar: a newspaper inside the siege of 1857 (Sun 27 Dec 2026) · History

Angle: a weekly Urdu newspaper printed in Delhi by Maulvi Muhammad Baqir kept publishing through the summer of 1857, while the city was held by the rebels and besieged by the British. What it reported, how a lithographed paper was made and read, why its pages are now a source for the siege, and what happened to its editor after the city fell. Open on one dated issue, not on the general story.

Leads found 17 Nov 2026 (confirm everything in the three checks; dates of founding and renaming vary between secondary accounts, so print only what a scholarly source states):
- Margrit Pernau, "The Delhi Urdu Akhbar: Between Persian Akhbarat and English Newspapers", Annual of Urdu Studies 18 (2003). https://minds.wisc.edu/items/7b1b5cbc-6a0f-497b-9103-0bccb17eee65
- Mahmood Farooqui (compiler and translator), Besieged: Voices from Delhi 1857 (Penguin Viking, 2010): translations from the Mutiny Papers, Persian and Urdu documents written inside the city.
- Look for: National Archives of India holdings of the paper; Pernau's later work on Delhi in the 1850s; C. M. Naim or Frances Pritchett on Muhammad Husain Azad (Baqir's son) only if it bears on the paper.
Avoid: hero or traitor framing; any quotation not read in a scholarly translation; the execution story beyond what a scholarly source states.
