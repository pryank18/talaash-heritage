# Durlabh #02 · The Ajanta copies that burned twice (Sun 29 Nov 2026) · Arts

Angle: Ajanta's 5th-century murals were copied in oil by Major Robert Gill (Madras Army) from 1844 to 1863 because the paintings were already fading. Most of his canvases burned at the Crystal Palace, Sydenham, in December 1866; a second set of copies (John Griffiths and the Bombay School of Art students, 1872–1885) was largely destroyed in a fire at the South Kensington Museum (now the V&A) in 1885. What survives, and why copies are now evidence of what the walls once showed.

Leads already seen (12 Nov; confirm each in the three checks, numbers differ between sources):
- V&A collection records (e.g. O115443, O115444, O115446): Gill commissioned 1844; "the 30 or so paintings he completed"; most destroyed at the Crystal Palace 1866; "one of only four surviving examples" at the V&A. https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O115444
- V&A Conservation Journal 52 (Spring 2006), "Conserving the copies of the Ajanta cave paintings at the V&A": Gill at Ajanta 1844–1863, about 30 large canvases, most destroyed 1866, four survive; June 1885 fire destroyed "well over a hundred canvases"; V&A holds 166 paintings today. https://www.vam.ac.uk/content/journals/conservation-journal/issue-52/conserving-the-copies-of-the-ajanta-cave-paintings-at-the-v-and-a

- Gill also photographed Ajanta (from the late 1850s/1860s); British Library holds his photographs and drawings.
