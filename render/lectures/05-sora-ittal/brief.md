# Durlabh #05 · Ittal: the wall paintings of the Sora (Saora) of Odisha (Sun 20 Dec 2026) · Anthropology

Angle: Sora (Saora, Savara) households in the hills of southern Odisha (Rayagada, Gajapati, Koraput districts) paint ittal / idital / anital on house walls, traditionally made for a particular spirit or ancestor at a shaman's direction. Verrier Elwin documented them in The Religion of an Indian Tribe (1955); Piers Vitebsky's ethnographies show how shamanism and the paintings have declined, while "Saora painting" is now made for sale. The piece is about what anthropologists learnt (a painting as a house for a spirit, made to be used, not admired) and about the ethics of turning ritual images into products.
Leads (confirm in checks): Elwin, The Religion of an Indian Tribe (OUP, 1955); Piers Vitebsky, Dialogues with the Dead (Cambridge UP; confirm year) and Living without the Dead (Chicago UP, 2017); MAP Academy article "Saora Paintings"; Orissa Review, Jan 2005, "Souras Paintings" (Odisha government) https://magazines.odisha.gov.in/orissareview/jan2005/englishPdf/Souras_Paintings.pdf ; Somen Rath, paper on Saora art and Alfred Gell's agency theory (serialsjournals.com).
Respect rules (Talaash anthropology standard): published ethnography only; no rites described in detail, nothing secret, no identifiable people or village photographs; the community's own terms first; say clearly that practice varies and has changed. Lanjia Saora are listed as a Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Group: state it only if confirmed from a government source.
