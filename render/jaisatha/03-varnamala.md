## Caption
Jaisa Tha #3 | The Sanskrit alphabet, a map of the mouth

Ka, kha, ga, gha, ṅa: this is where the consonants of the Sanskrit varṇamālā begin, and the order is not random. The first 25 consonants come in five rows, grouped by where the mouth makes them, and the rows run from the back of the mouth to the lips.

• A phonetics text ascribed to Pāṇini, the Pāṇinīya Śikṣā, names eight places of sound: chest, throat, head, root of the tongue, teeth, nose, lips and palate. Its date is disputed
• Velar: k, kh, g, gh, ṅ, with the back of the tongue, furthest back in the mouth
• Palatal: c, ch, j, jh, ñ, with the flat of the tongue against the hard palate
• Retroflex: ṭ, ṭh, ḍ, ḍh, ṇ, with the tip of the tongue turned up and drawn back into the dome of the palate
• Dental: t, th, d, dh, n, with the tip of the tongue at the teeth
• Labial: p, ph, b, bh, m, at the lips

Across each row the place stays the same and only breath and voice change: k, then kh with a puff of breath, g voiced, gh voiced with breath, and ṅ through the nose. This is the traditional order of the consonant rows in the Sanskrit alphabet.

The 3D head is an illustrative section, not to scale. The row names are modern phonetic terms, and the letters are typed, not traced from a manuscript.
