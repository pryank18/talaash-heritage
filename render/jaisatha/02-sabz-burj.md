## Caption
Jaisa Tha #2 | Sabz Burj, Delhi

On a roundabout at the entrance to Humayun's Tomb stands Sabz Burj, an octagonal tomb in the Timurid style. It bears no date, and no one recorded who is buried here; its style places it in the early Mughal years.

• Glazed tiles: today the lotus finial is green and the dome turquoise blue, and the drum carries blues, green and yellow. Sources differ on the dome's original colour
• Early 20th century: the tomb was used as a police station, and its painted interior was possibly plastered over then
• 1980s: the dome was re-clad in terracotta tiles, and water seeping through the dome permanently damaged the edge of the painted ceiling
• Conservation by the Aga Khan Trust for Culture with the Archaeological Survey of India, finished in 2021, removed the cement and uncovered a 36 m² domed ceiling painted with pure gold and lapis lazuli

The ceiling's five main colours: lapis blue for the ground of the flower design, gold over a beige undercoat, red, yellow earth (peeli maati), and black for the bold lines. It is thought to be among the earliest surviving painted ceilings in India.

The 3D scenes are reconstructions from the published conservation records, not to scale. The floral pattern is not shown.

## Notes (own check, 14 Nov 2026)
- Date: S1 and S2 say the style dates to the 1530s; S4 says "1520s". The sources disagree, so no decade is given; "early Mughal years" only. S1: "bears no date and it is not recorded who lies buried here".
- Conservation start: 2017 (S1, S2) or 2018 (S4, S5). Disagreement, so only the finish (2021, all sources) is given.
- "Earliest": S1 "amongst the earliest surviving painted ceiling in India" and "earliest existing painted ceiling for a Mughal era structure"; S2/S3/S4 "thought to be the earliest surviving painted ceiling for any monument in India". The reel uses the weaker "among the earliest".
- Tiles: S1 "lotus finial ... green tiles while the dome has turquoise blue"; "drum has a repetitive pattern of lapiz blue, turquoise blue green and yellow – all in equal measure". S5 gives a slightly different drum list (yellow, green, two blues); S1, the conservators' own leaflet, is followed. The drum is shown as simple bands of the three colours: "pattern simplified".
- Police station: S1 "used as a Police Station in the early 20th century"; the plastering of the painted interior "possibly when the structure was used as a police station", hence "probably".
- 1984: S1 caption (courtesy Ebba Koch) "By 1984, much of the tiles on the dome had fallen or lost their glaze".
- 1980s tiles: S1 "in the 1980's the ASI removed all existing tiles from the dome and replaced these with lapiz blue tiles"; "made with terracotta rather than using a quartz base used by the 16th century builders". S2: water seepage from the 1980s tiling threatened the painted ceiling.
- Ceiling: S1 "The 36 square meter domed ceiling has five prominent colours, lapiz blue forms the background for the flower design"; "pure gold that has survived in specks was applied over a beige undercoat"; "yellow earth or peeli maati"; "Black is used prominently for the bold and confident lines". Red: S1 names red ochre and red lead, so the reel says only "red". 36 m² × 10.764 = 387.5 sq ft, "about 390 sq ft".
- Missing parts: S1/S2 not restored; S1 "architect Himanish Das has produced a painting depicting the full extent of the painted ceiling" (the reel says "a drawing"; S1 also captions it "Reconstruction drawing").
- Façades: S3/S4 each of the eight facades has a different incised plaster pattern; S3 "geometric, floral, and inscriptions"; fragments survived, so they were restored.
- No figures, no imagined pattern: the ceiling is a plain lapis ground with gold specks marked as illustrative; the façade patterns are not drawn.
