## Caption
Jaisa Tha #1 | Purana Qila

Delhi's Old Fort stands on a mound people have lived on for over 2,500 years. Archaeological Survey of India excavations have recorded nine cultural levels, from pre-Mauryan to Mughal:

• Pre-Mauryan: Painted Grey Ware sherds below the Mauryan floors; dated samples from this level go back as early as 540 BCE
• Mauryan: a ring well of terracotta rings
• Sunga, Kushan and Gupta: mud-brick and burnt-brick buildings, copper coins and sealings
• Post-Gupta and Rajput: brick-bat and stone walls, and a stone image of Vishnu
• Sultanate and Mughal: lime floors, glazed ware, and the fort. Humayun founded his city Dinpanah here in 1533; it passed to Sher Shah when he took Delhi in 1540

Excavation has gone almost 14 m deep, and natural soil has still not been reached.

The 3D scenes are reconstructions from the published reports, not to scale.

## Notes
- Period spellings follow the sources: Sunga (S1, S2), Kushan (S1).
- Never shorten 540 BCE to "PGW dated to 540 BCE": the date is from organic samples of the level.
- Indraprastha is deliberately not mentioned (contested identification).
- Vishnu image: S1 text and PIB call it stone; one S1 figure caption says terracotta. "Stone" follows the text.
- Rajput wall: S1 gives 4.40 × 1.40 × 1 m; "possible fortification wall".
- Site illustration rule: no deity images. The Vishnu image is shown as a plain, covered stele shape with the label, not a figure.
