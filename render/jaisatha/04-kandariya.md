## Caption
Jaisa Tha #4 | Kandariya Mahadeva, Khajuraho

The largest standing temple at Khajuraho, Madhya Pradesh, rebuilt in 3D from Alexander Cunningham's survey. Krishna Deva of the Archaeological Survey of India dates it to about 1025–50 CE and, as a plausible inference, to the latter part of the reign of the Chandela king Vidyādhara; the ASI's website attributes it to King Gaṇḍa.

• Measured by Cunningham: 109 ft (33.2 m) long, about 59½ ft (18.1 m) wide, 116½ ft (35.5 m) high above the ground and 88 ft (26.8 m) above its own floor
• Plan: porch, hall, great hall with transepts, vestibule and sanctum, a large double cross, with three open porticoes lighting the passage round the sanctum
• Roofs: each part has its own pinnacled roof, rising from the low entrance roof to the lofty spire, which carries an ascending series of 84 smaller replicas of itself
• Inside: a great hall with four central pillars, and a narrow passage round the sanctum, where a marble liṅga stands
• Sculpture: Cunningham counted 226 statues inside and 646 outside, 872 in all, carved in buff, pink or pale-yellow sandstone

The 3D scenes are a reconstruction from published surveys and plans. Estimated parts (terrace height, chamber sizes, the grouping of the smaller spires) are tagged, and the sculpture is shown as plain blocks.
