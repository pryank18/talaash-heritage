# Sources: What the Text Says #01, "Where did 'zero' come from?"
Every claim in the script, captions and on-screen text, with its source. Opened 14 Nov 2026.

| # | Claim (as used) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | English took "zero" from Italian, directly or through French. | Merriam-Webster, "zero", etymology: "French or Italian; French zéro, from Italian zero". https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/zero |
| C2 | Italian "zero" comes from Medieval Latin "zephirum". | Same: "from Italian zero, from Medieval Latin zephirum". |
| C3 | "zephirum" comes from Arabic ṣifr. | Same: "from Medieval Latin zephirum, from Arabic ṣifr". |
| C4 | ṣifr means "empty". | Merriam-Webster, "cipher", etymology: "Arabic ṣifr empty, cipher, zero"; K. Jaouiche, "India's Contribution to Arab Mathematics" (in D. Tournès ed., L'Océan Indien au carrefour des mathématiques arabes, chinoises, européennes et indiennes, IUFM de La Réunion, 1998, pp. 211–223; English tr. D. Karanth, arXiv:1008.0874, 2010), p. 5: "the word sifr ... has the meaning 'empty' in Arabic". |
| C5 | "cipher" comes from that same Arabic word, through Medieval Latin "cifra". | Merriam-Webster, "cipher": "Middle English, from Medieval Latin cifra, from Arabic ṣifr". https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/cipher |
| C6 | Merriam-Webster still lists "zero" as the first meaning of "cipher" (on-screen card and captions; not narrated). | Same, noun sense 1a: "zero". |
| C7 | ṣifr was itself a translation: it translated Sanskrit śūnya, which also means "empty". | Jaouiche (1998; arXiv:1008.0874), p. 5: sifr "the Arabic translation of the Sanskrit 'śunya', which has precisely the same meaning". |
| C8 | Apte's Sanskrit dictionary gives śūnya as "empty, void", and also as "a cipher". | V. S. Apte, The Practical Sanskrit-English Dictionary (Poona, 1890), p. 1052, s.v. śūnya: adj. 1 "Empty, void"; n. 1 "A vacuum, void, blank"; n. 3 "A cipher, dot." Scan: https://sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/scans/AP90Scan/AP90Scanpdf/pg_1052.pdf. Check B confirmed the same senses in the revised Apte (Gode and Karve, 1957–59) and Monier-Williams, Sanskrit-English Dictionary (1899): "empty, void"; "(in arithm.) nought, a cypher". |
| C9 | So zero and cipher both come from one Arabic word that translated a Sanskrit word for "empty". | C1–C7 together (both words: Merriam-Webster; translation: Jaouiche p. 5). |
| P | Talaash page: Sanskrit and Indian knowledge systems guide. | https://talaash-heritage.pages.dev/sanskrit-and-indian-knowledge-systems (opened 14 Nov 2026; live; mentions the place-value decimal system with zero). |
