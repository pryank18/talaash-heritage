What the Text Says #01 | Where did "zero" come from?

Follow the word backwards. Merriam-Webster traces English "zero" to Italian zero (directly or through French zéro), then to Medieval Latin zephirum, and then to the Arabic word ṣifr, "empty".

English has a second word from the same Arabic word: "cipher", through Medieval Latin cifra. Merriam-Webster still lists "zero" as its first meaning.

And ṣifr was itself a translation. Khalil Jaouiche, in "India's Contribution to Arab Mathematics" (1998, p. 5), notes that ṣifr is the Arabic translation of the Sanskrit śūnya, which has the same meaning. V. S. Apte's Practical Sanskrit-English Dictionary (1890, p. 1052) gives śūnya as "empty, void" and also as "a cipher".

Two English words, one Arabic word, and behind it a Sanskrit word for "empty".
