## Narration (as captioned on screen; final after the three checks)
1. Why do we call nothing "zero"? Let's follow the word backwards.
2. English took "zero" from Italian, directly or through French.
3. Italian "zero" comes from Medieval Latin "zephirum".
4. And "zephirum" comes from an Arabic word, ṣifr, meaning "empty".
5. English has a second word from that same Arabic word: "cipher".
6. It came through Medieval Latin "cifra".
7. But the Arabic word ṣifr was itself a translation.
8. It translated the Sanskrit word śūnya, which also means "empty".
9. Apte's Sanskrit dictionary gives śūnya as "empty, void", and also as "a cipher".
10. So zero and cipher both come from one Arabic word that translated a Sanskrit word for "empty".
