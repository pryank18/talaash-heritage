Where did the word "zero" come from?

Merriam-Webster traces English "zero" to Italian zero (directly or through French), then to Medieval Latin zephirum, and then to the Arabic ṣifr, "empty". "Cipher" comes from the same Arabic word, through Medieval Latin cifra, and its first listed meaning is still "zero".

ṣifr was itself a translation: Khalil Jaouiche ("India's Contribution to Arab Mathematics", 1998, p. 5) notes that it is the Arabic translation of the Sanskrit śūnya, which has the same meaning. Apte's Sanskrit-English Dictionary (1890, p. 1052) gives śūnya as "empty, void" and "a cipher".
