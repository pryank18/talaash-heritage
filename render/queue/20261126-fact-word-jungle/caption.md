# Fact 015 · Word story · jungle · Thu 26 Nov 2026, evening slot (row label 19:30) · Linguistics

## Caption
"Jungle" is a word from India, and its Sanskrit source meant dry land, not forest.

English "jungle" comes from Hindi-Urdu jangal (जंगल), and that from Sanskrit jaṅgala. Merriam-Webster glosses the Sanskrit word as "desert region".

Hobson-Jobson, the old glossary of Anglo-Indian words, notes that the Sanskrit word occurs chiefly in medical treatises: Suśruta sorts land into humid, jaṅgala and ordinary. In strictness, it says, the Indian word meant only waste, uncultivated ground.

Merriam-Webster dates the first known use in English to 1776. In that year Halhed's Code of Gentoo Laws said: "Land Waste for Five Years, or whatever longer Time it may happen, such Land is called Jungle." Only later did English use it for dense forest and tangled growth.
