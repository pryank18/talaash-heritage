# Deep Story · the Bower Manuscript · Wed 25 Nov 2026, evening slot (row label 19:30) · Sanskrit

## Caption
Medicine, dice oracles and protective charms, written in Sanskrit on birch bark and found near Kucha, an oasis town on the Silk Road in what is now western China.

This is the Bower Manuscript, named after Lieutenant H. H. Bower, the British officer who obtained it from local treasure-seekers. The Bodleian Library counts 54 folios, written in an early Gupta script by four different scribes, and calls it one of the oldest surviving Sanskrit manuscripts.

Its seven texts range from collections of medical formulae to fortune-telling with dice. The Bodleian dates it to the 5th–6th century CE; other estimates vary. Rudolf Hoernle deciphered it, and his edition was published in Calcutta in parts from 1893. The Bodleian bought it in 1898; in Oxford it is MS. Sansk. c. 17.
