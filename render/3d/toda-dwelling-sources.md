## The object
The barrel-roofed dwelling hut of the Toda people of the Nilgiri hills, as recorded in published ethnography: a vernacular house type you can turn and cut open. Dwelling only. The Toda dairies are sacred places and are not modelled; no people are shown other than the plain 1.7 m scale figure.

## Sources to use (primary or standard works)
- W. H. R. Rivers, *The Todas* (London, 1906): chapter on the village and the hut, with drawings and photographs (public domain).
- M. B. Emeneau, published work on the Toda language and culture, for the names of the parts of the house (cite volume and page).
- Any later published survey of Toda houses (for example in the *Anthropological Survey of India* series) that gives measurements; cite it, and tag figures from it as Measured.

## Check 1 extraction, 12 Nov 2026 (short OCR quotes; verify against the page images)
Sources: W. H. R. Rivers, *The Todas* (1906; fieldwork 1901-02), Project Gutenberg 71828; J. W. Breeks, *An Account of the Primitive Tribes and Monuments of the Nilagiris* (1873), quoting Dr Shortt; W. E. Marshall, *A Phrenologist amongst the Todas* (1873), ch. VI "The Mand", pp. 58ff.
- Name (Documented, Rivers p. 28): the house is *ars* (Marshall: *Arsh*); village wall *katu* (Rivers) but *Tuar* (Marshall): show both.
- Shape (Documented, Rivers p. 28): "shaped like half a barrel", roof and sides projecting beyond the front partition with the door.
- Size: Rivers gives none ("by no means constant"). Shortt (via Breeks): "usually 10 feet high, 18 feet long, and 9 feet broad"; inside "from 8 to 15 feet square". Marshall: rooms "five to six cubits" square and high; his plan "8 ft. long, 8 ft. broad, and 8 ft. high". Show as a range with both sources (Measured, older accounts, about 1870).
- Door (Documented): very small, one enters on all fours; Shortt "32 inches in height and 18 in width" (OCR, verify); Marshall "about two cubits high by one and a half cubits broad"; closed by a sliding slab of wood or stone.
- Materials (Documented, Shortt and Marshall): bamboo laid close and fastened with rattan, thatched; end walls of solid wood planking, gaps filled with clay and cow-dung. Rivers is silent on materials; no stone front in any source.
- Inside (Documented): raised sleeping platforms *tün* (Rivers p. 30); Shortt: clay platform about 2 ft high on one side, fireplace on the other; floor divided by the pounding hole (Rivers p. 30; Shortt: 7 to 9 in deep). Marshall's plan gives a store 4½ × 2½ ft and a raised clay bed 8 × 3½ ft (OCR fractions, verify).
- Outside (Documented): raised seats *kwottün* either side of the door (Rivers p. 29); enclosure wall of loose stones 2-3 ft (Shortt), about 3 ft (Marshall).
- Period: the measured figures are from about 1870, the description from 1901-02. Say so on the page; the Todas live in the Nilgiris today and houses have changed.
- Not modelled: dairies (sacred), people.
