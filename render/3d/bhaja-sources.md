# Bhaja Caves, Cave 12 chaitya hall: 3D evidence sheet

Copied 11 Nov 2026 from the Design canvas "Bhaja Caves – 3D Reconstruction" (artifact 478bfafe-eaef-4987-bd7d-5d54414d37e2, version 1794390555-e4b8). Same text as the project note "bhaja-3d-sources".

## Sources named in the model
J. Burgess & J. Fergusson, *The Cave Temples of India* (1880); *Gazetteer of the Bombay Presidency*, Poona volume (1885); Archaeological Survey of India. Comparative: stupa-worship reliefs at Bharhut (Freer Gallery of Art) and Sanchi; Ajanta pigment studies.

## Measured (Burgess 1880 / Gazetteer 1885)
- Date: 2nd century BCE; among the earliest surviving chaitya halls.
- Faces west, cut into the scarp about 400 ft (about 122 m) above Bhaja village.
- Length 59 ft (17.98 m), front to back of the apse. Width 26 ft 8 in (8.13 m) (Burgess); the 1885 Gazetteer gives about 29 ft.
- Height 26 ft 5 in (8.05 m), floor to the crown of the vault.
- 27 plain octagonal pillars, 11 ft 4 in (3.45 m) high, raking inward about 5 in (13 cm).
- Nave 16 ft 4 in (4.98 m) wide at the base, 15 ft 6 in (4.72 m) at the pillar tops; aisle 3 ft 5 in (1.04 m).
- Vault springs from a ledge 7 ft 5 in (2.26 m) above the pillar tops.
- Teak ribs line the vault; the first four and parts of others had fallen by the 1880s.
- Stupa 11 ft (3.35 m) across at the floor; drum 4 ft, dome 6 ft; two-storeyed box capital; upper box has a hole 7 in wide and 1 ft 8 in deep for a wooden umbrella shaft.
- Timber front: four floor sockets for uprights, rock mortices for a cross-beam, about 170 pin holes in three rows on the great arch.

## Reconstructed / estimated
- Timber front form (hypothetical): screen with central and side doors, H-frame and lattice in the arch window, umbrella.
- Estimated: pillar spacing (11 a side, 5 round the apse), rib spacing, box-capital sizes, aisle ceiling, facade relief, terrain.

## Layers
- In use (illustrative): monks walk clockwise round the stupa; others sit in the nave; donors bring flowers; lamps at the stupa.
- No Buddha image (documented): worship centred on the stupa; painted Buddha images on some pillars are much later (up to about the 5th–6th century CE).
- Colour and rituals (comparative, Bharhut early 2nd century BCE and Sanchi 1st century BCE reliefs): garlands, strewn flowers, kneeling, touching the stupa base, banners, musicians; hand prints on a stupa base. Not recorded at Bhaja itself.
- Painted facade (hypothetical). Palette (comparative): red and yellow ochre, lime white, lamp black, as in Ajanta Caves 9 and 10. Interior polished stone (documented).
- Chant: "Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa" three times and the Three Refuges open the Khuddakapāṭha; homage also three times in MN 100. Words, language and melody at Bhaja are not recorded.
