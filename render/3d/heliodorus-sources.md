## The object
A stone pillar at Besnagar near Vidisha, Madhya Pradesh, with a Brahmi inscription naming Heliodorus, an envoy of the Indo-Greek king Antialkidas. Model: the shaft and capital as a stone you can turn, with the inscription's letters drawn after a published plate, letter by letter, and a transliteration panel.

## Sources to use (primary or standard works)
- J. Marshall, "Excavations at Besnagar", *Archaeological Survey of India Annual Report 1908-09* (and the 1913-14 report by D. R. Bhandarkar): pillar measurements, base excavation, photographs.
- D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions Bearing on Indian History and Civilization*, vol. I (1942; 2nd ed. 1965): text and reading of the inscription.

- Capital (Documented, p. 126): "of the Persepolitan bell-shaped type, with a massive abacus surmounting it"; the palm-leaf crown may not belong to the pillar; the top "probably bore the effigy of" Garuda (Hypothetical: model as a plain labelled block, no figure).

- Inscription A (Documented, p. 128): seven lines; names Heliodoros son of Dion, of Taxila, Greek ambassador of King Antialkidas to King (Kasiputra) Bhagabhadra, in his 14th regnal year; the Garuda standard of Vasudeva, "god of gods". Readings of line 4 differed (Bloch, Fleet, Venis): show Vogel's reading and say so.
- Inscription B (Documented, p. 128): two lines on the other side: "three steps to immortality ... self-control, self-denial and watchfulness".

- Date (Documented): Antialkidas about the middle of the 2nd century BCE (AR 1908-09 p. 127, after Gardner; Marshall, JRAS 1909: c. 140 BCE). Show as a range with the sources.
