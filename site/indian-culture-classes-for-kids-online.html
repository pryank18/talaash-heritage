Taught by a historian, not read from a script

Your child's guide is a doctoral researcher in ancient Indian history, culture and archaeology at Deccan College, Pune, who has taught Indian culture and stories to children of Indian families living abroad. Every story is told the way children listen and checked against the texts and the evidence, so what your child learns is accurate as well as memorable.

Classes never go above four children. The guide knows each child: what they already know, what makes them laugh, and the question they asked last week. Every class is live; nothing is a recorded video.

For third-culture kids who move between two worlds every day, it gives the Indian one stories they can call their own. The classes are run by Talaash Heritage, a team of archaeologists, linguists, anthropologists and historians working together since 2018. See our work.

Two age groups, two journeys

A five-year-old needs adventure; a fourteen-year-old needs answers. So children learn with others their own age.

  • Little Explorers, ages 5 to 9Stories first. 45-minute classes full of the Ramayana, the Panchatantra and the Jataka tales, festivals and why we keep them, and first shlokas. Pictures, voices and play, and no pressure to read or write.
  • Young Seekers, ages 10 to 14Meaning and roots. 60-minute classes that read the Ramayana and the Mahabharata for their big questions, open up the Vedas and the rishis, map India's regions and languages, and talk honestly about growing up between two cultures.

Ramayana, shlokas and the Vedas: the first term

A term is twelve weeks. Every class has the same shape: a story or an idea, a short practice such as a shloka, a word or a drawing, and one thing to carry home.

Ages 5 to 9 · Term 1

Our stories

  1. The gods and goddesses of the stories, and what each one stands for
  2. The Ramayana, told as an adventure over several weeks
  3. The Panchatantra and the Jataka tales: animals and their lessons
  4. Festivals and why we celebrate them
  5. First shlokas and the sounds of Sanskrit

Ages 10 to 14 · Term 1

Where we come from

  1. What the four Vedas are, and how they were kept by memory for centuries before they were written down
  2. The rishis: who they were and what the tradition remembers of them
  3. The Ramayana and the Mahabharata, read for their big questions
  4. India on the map: regions, languages, scripts and history
  5. Being Indian abroad: identity, and an honest conversation about it

Later terms

Going deeper

  1. The Bhagavad Gita and its big ideas, from age 10
  2. Mathematics and astronomy in ancient India
  3. Temples, art and how to read them
  4. Archaeology: how we know what we know about ancient India, from Harappan cities to Ashoka's edicts

Questions parents ask

How is this different from Bal Vihar or a temple class?

Temple and Bal Vihar classes are community programmes, usually in larger groups, and many families value them. Ours is a private class: never more than four children, one guide who follows your child's progress, a time you choose, and a written report each month. Your child can do both.

Can children in Dubai and the UAE join?

Yes. The UAE is an hour and a half behind India, so Saturday and Sunday mornings and afternoons in Dubai work well. Families in the UK, Europe, North America, South-East Asia and Australia can join too; we set the time with you.

Does my child need to know Hindi or Sanskrit?

No. Classes are in English. Hindi and Sanskrit words, names and shlokas are introduced a few at a time, with their meaning, so your child picks them up along the way.

Do you teach the Bhagavad Gita to kids?

Yes, from age 10. Its main ideas come in a later term for Young Seekers, after the Mahabharata, the epic the Gita belongs to, has given them the story it grows out of.

What happens in the free trial class?

A 30-minute live class, one-to-one, in which your child learns one complete story and can retell it at home that evening. You join for the last few minutes to talk about the options and any questions. There is no obligation to continue.

Is this religious instruction?

It is an education in India's stories, texts, history and customs: what they say, where they come from and what they have meant to people. Shlokas are learnt as language and heritage. Families of every background are welcome, and you can tell us what matters most to you.

How will I know my child is learning?

You receive a short note after every class with something to talk about at home, a written progress report each month, and an invitation to a showcase at the end of each term where your child tells a story, recites what they have learnt or presents a small project.

How are the fees paid?

Fees are in US dollars, per child, per month, for eight live classes. Before the first paid class we confirm the plan and the fee in writing and send a payment link. Families who pay for a full term of three months in advance get a lower rate.
