A guide, not a class

Your child's guide is a doctoral researcher in ancient Indian history, culture and archaeology at Deccan College, Pune, who has taught Indian culture and stories to children of Indian families living abroad. The sessions are built on the texts and the evidence, and told the way children listen.

Groups never go above four, so the guide knows each child: what they already know, what makes them laugh, and the question they asked last week. Every session is live. Nothing is a recorded lecture.

The programme is run by Talaash Heritage, a team of archaeologists, linguists, anthropologists and historians working together since 2018. See our work.

Two age groups

A five-year-old and a fourteen-year-old need different stories, pace and questions, so children learn with others of their own age.

  • Little Explorers, ages 5 to 9Stories first. 45-minute sessions built on the Ramayana, the Panchatantra and the Jataka tales, festivals and why we keep them, and first shlokas. Pictures, voices and play, and no pressure to read or write.
  • Young Seekers, ages 10 to 14Meaning and roots. 60-minute sessions that read the epics for their big questions, explain what the Vedas are and who the rishis were, map India's regions, languages and history, and talk honestly about growing up between two cultures.

What they learn in the first term

A term is twelve weeks. Each session has the same shape: a story or an idea, a short practice such as a shloka, a word or a drawing, and one thing to carry home.

Ages 5 to 9 · Term 1

Our stories

  1. The gods and goddesses of the stories, and what each one stands for
  2. The Ramayana, told as an adventure over several weeks
  3. The Panchatantra and the Jataka tales: animals and their lessons
  4. Festivals and why we celebrate them
  5. First shlokas and the sounds of Sanskrit

Ages 10 to 14 · Term 1

Where we come from

  1. What the four Vedas are, and how they were kept by memory for centuries before they were written down
  2. The rishis: who they were and what the tradition remembers of them
  3. The Ramayana and the Mahabharata, read for their big questions
  4. India on the map: regions, languages, scripts and history
  5. Being Indian abroad: identity, and an honest conversation about it

Later terms

Going deeper

  1. The ideas of the Bhagavad Gita
  2. Mathematics and astronomy in ancient India
  3. Temples, art and how to read them
  4. Archaeology: how we know what we know about ancient India, from Harappan cities to Ashoka's edicts

Questions parents ask

Does my child need to know Hindi or Sanskrit?

No. Sessions are in English. Hindi and Sanskrit words, names and shlokas are introduced a few at a time, with their meaning, so your child picks them up along the way.

What happens in the free taster?

A 30-minute live session, one-to-one, in which your child learns one complete story and can retell it at home that evening. You join for the last few minutes to talk about the options and any questions. There is no obligation to continue.

Which time zones do you teach in?

Sessions are on Saturdays and Sundays, at a time set in your time zone. Families anywhere can join: the Gulf, the UK and Europe, North America, South-East Asia and Australia.

Is this religious instruction?

It is an education in India's stories, texts, history and customs: what they say, where they come from and what they have meant to people. Shlokas are learnt as language and heritage. Families of every background are welcome, and you can tell us what matters most to you.

How will I know my child is learning?

You receive a short note after every session with something to talk about at home, a written progress note each month, and an invitation to a showcase at the end of each term where your child tells a story, recites what they have learnt or presents a small project.

How are the fees paid?

Fees are in US dollars, per child, per month, for eight live sessions. Before the first paid session we confirm the plan and the fee in writing and send a payment link. Families who pay for a full term of three months in advance get a lower rate.
